# Statins for Healthy Adults Over 70 Without Cardiovascular Disease

This is genuinely contested in the evidence base. Here's where things stand:

## The honest answer: uncertain, lean toward shared decision-making

There's no clean "yes" or "no" — the evidence for **primary prevention** (no prior CVD) in this age group is thinner than most people assume.

## What the evidence shows

**Limitations of the data:**
- Most major statin RCTs (e.g., JUPITER, HOPE-3) under-enrolled adults over 70–75, especially those over 80.
- The 2019 Lancet meta-analysis (CTT Collaboration) found statins reduce major vascular events across age groups, but the relative benefit *appeared* attenuated in over-75s without prior CVD — though confidence intervals were wide.
- The ongoing **STAREE** (Australia) and **PREVENTABLE** (US) trials are specifically designed to answer this question; results are not yet available.

**What guidelines say (and they disagree):**
- **USPSTF (2022):** Insufficient evidence to recommend for or against initiating statins in adults ≥76 for primary prevention.
- **ACC/AHA (2018):** Reasonable to consider in 75+ after clinician–patient risk discussion.
- **NICE (UK):** Offer atorvastatin 20 mg if 10-year QRISK ≥10%, with caveats about life expectancy and frailty in older adults.

## Factors that shift the calculation

**Toward starting:**
- Elevated LDL, hypertension, diabetes, smoking, family history
- Good functional status and life expectancy >5–10 years
- Coronary artery calcium score >100 (increasingly used to refine risk)

**Against starting:**
- Frailty, limited life expectancy, polypharmacy
- History of statin intolerance
- Patient preference after informed discussion
- Cognitive concerns about pill burden / adherence

## Bottom line

For a *healthy*, *robust* 70-something with elevated CV risk factors, a statin is **reasonable** — but not automatic. For a frail 80-something with no CVD and limited life expectancy, the benefit-to-burden ratio likely doesn't favor it. The decision should hinge on individualized risk estimation, life expectancy, patient values, and a candid conversation about the modest absolute risk reduction in this population.

This is information, not medical advice — the actual decision belongs to the patient and their clinician.
