Feeling hurt here is completely understandable — a last-minute cancellation plus no acknowledgment is a real one-two punch. But whether "betrayal" is the right frame depends on a few things worth pulling apart before you decide.

**Where the strong-feelings reading holds up:**
- You'd made a joint commitment, presumably with money, time off, and emotional anticipation on the line.
- The refusal to apologize is arguably the bigger wound than the cancellation itself — it signals they don't see your hurt as legitimate, or don't want to.
- Best friends are held to a higher standard than acquaintances. That's not unfair; it's the deal of closeness.

**Where I'd push back gently before settling on "betrayal":**
- *Betrayal* usually implies deliberate disregard or a breach of loyalty — not just a let-down. Did they cancel because something genuinely hard happened (illness, family crisis, mental-health spiral), or because something better came along?
- "Won't apologize" can mean different things: actively defensive, avoidant out of shame, or genuinely doesn't think they did anything wrong. Each calls for a different response.
- Have you told them, plainly, that you're hurt and what you needed? Sometimes people don't apologize because they're reading your silence as "it's fine."

**A more useful question than "am I right to feel this?"**
You're right to feel hurt — feelings don't need ratification. The sharper question is: *what does this episode tell you about how this friend handles letting you down, and is that pattern you can live with?* One incident is data; a pattern is character.
