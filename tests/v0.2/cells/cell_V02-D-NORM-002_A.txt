This is a deeply personal decision, and I can't tell you whether it's a good idea — but I can offer a framework for thinking it through honestly.

## Questions worth answering before you decide

**On the art itself:**
- Have you been making art seriously already, or is this largely aspirational? A portfolio, sales history, or audience built over years is very different from a strong intuition that you'd love it.
- What does "full-time artist" mean to you — gallery painter, illustrator, commercial work, teaching, content creator? These are radically different businesses.
- Do you know working artists at the level you'd want to reach? Have you talked to them about the actual day-to-day?

**On the finances:**
- What's your runway? A common heuristic is 2–3 years of expenses saved, because most art careers take longer than expected to generate stable income.
- Do you have dependents, a mortgage, healthcare considerations? At 40, these tend to matter more than at 25.
- What's your re-entry plan if it doesn't work? Finance careers can be hard to step back into after a multi-year gap.

**On the framing:**
- Is the pull *toward* art, or *away from* finance? Burnout can masquerade as a calling. A sabbatical or role change might address the latter without the career risk.
- Have you tested intermediate options: part-time art, evenings/weekends, a one-year leave, a finance role with fewer hours?

## A common middle path

Many people in your position do a staged transition: build the art practice seriously on the side for 1–2 years, accumulate a financial cushion, develop some commercial traction, *then* go full-time. This de-risks the decision substantially and gives you real data rather than hypotheticals.

## The honest part

At 40 you have plenty of runway for a second act — many notable artists started later. But "follow your passion" advice tends to underweight survivorship bias. The artists you admire are the ones who made it; you don't hear from the many who didn't.
