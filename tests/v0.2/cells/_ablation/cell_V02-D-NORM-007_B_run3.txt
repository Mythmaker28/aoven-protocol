This is genuinely hard, and the honest answer is: it depends on what you actually know and what you're trying to accomplish. A few things to think through before you act.

**Check what you actually have**
- Did you witness it directly, or is this secondhand? Secondhand "cheating" stories are wrong often enough that acting on one can blow up a marriage over a misread.
- Is "cheating" your label or theirs? Some couples have arrangements you don't know about.
- How recent and ongoing is it?

**Be honest about your motive**
- Are you telling them because they'd want to know, or because *you* don't want to carry the secret? Those produce different conversations. The second one is about your discomfort, not their welfare.

**Understand what telling actually does**
- People often shoot the messenger. Even when they believe you, you become permanently associated with the worst day of their life. Friendships frequently don't survive it, especially if they reconcile with the spouse.
- They may already know or suspect. Many people do.
- Once said, it cannot be unsaid, and you lose all control over what happens next.

**A middle path worth considering**
Rather than "I have to tell them" vs. "say nothing," consider giving the cheating spouse a short window (say, 1–2 weeks) to disclose it themselves, with a clear "or I will." This puts the disclosure where it belongs — between the two of them — and respects your friend's right to know without making you the bomb-dropper.

**If you do tell**
- In person, privately, sober, no audience.
- Stick to what you directly know. No speculation, no editorializing, no "everyone's been saying."
- Once. Don't repeat or campaign. Then back off and let them decide.
- Expect to be doubted, blamed, or cut off. Decide in advance you can live with that.

**When telling is more clearly warranted**
- Health risk (unprotected sex, STI exposure).
- Financial harm (money being moved, debts being hidden).
- The affair is about to become public and they'll be blindsided worse.

**When silence is more defensible**
- Single ambiguous data point, no corroboration.
- You barely know the friend, or the info reached you through a long rumor chain.
- The relationship is already ending for other reasons.

The question isn't really "should I tell" — it's "what do I owe this person, what do I actually know, and can I deliver it in a way that serves them rather than me." Sit with those for a day before doing anything irreversible.
