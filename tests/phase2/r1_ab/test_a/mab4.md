# MAB-4 / Test A (baseline, no Aoven)

**Prompt:** Will room-temperature ambient-pressure superconductors be discovered in the next decade?

---

Probably not, but it's not impossible. As of 2024, no material has been independently and reproducibly demonstrated to superconduct at room temperature and ambient pressure. The hydride family (LK-99, hydrogen sulfide, lanthanum hydride) either failed reproduction or required extreme pressures above 100 GPa to work. The current ambient-pressure record is held by cuprates, with critical temperatures around 138 K — well below room temperature.

The 2023 LK-99 episode is instructive. A claimed room-temperature ambient-pressure superconductor triggered a global rapid-reproduction effort that found multiple independent failures within weeks. The community is now primed for fast verification, which both lowers the discovery time (claims get tested fast) and raises the credibility bar (no more decade-long rumor cycles).

Theoretical mechanisms for room-temperature superconductivity at ambient pressure exist on paper, but no candidate material has the kind of quantitative theoretical backing that would justify high confidence in imminent discovery. ML-driven materials search and autonomous-lab efforts could compress the timeline if a productive search direction is identified, but no such direction is publicly singled out as imminent.

A reasonable estimate is something like 15-30% probability of discovery within the next decade, based on the rate of incremental Tc improvement over the past 30 years. Even if discovered, scaling to manufacturable wire/film is a separate 10-20 year engineering problem. So planning grid and electronics roadmaps assuming current cuprate/iron-pnictide materials remain the operational ceiling for the next decade is the prudent baseline.
