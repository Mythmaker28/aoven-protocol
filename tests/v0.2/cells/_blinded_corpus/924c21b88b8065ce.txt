This is a personal decision that depends a lot on your situation, but here are some things worth thinking through:

**Questions to ask yourself:**
- Can you actually afford it without straining your own finances, savings, or goals?
- Is the money going toward genuine emergencies/necessities, or recurring lifestyle costs?
- Has anything changed over time — is he working toward stability, or has the pattern deepened?
- How do *you* feel after each request? Resentful, anxious, fine?
- Are there strings or expectations (spoken or unspoken) attached on either side?

**Some patterns to watch for:**
- **Crisis vs. cycle:** One-time help during a real setback is very different from ongoing subsidy. If each "emergency" is followed by another, the money may not be solving the underlying problem.
- **Enabling vs. supporting:** Money can sometimes delay someone from confronting a job, spending, or addiction issue. Support isn't always financial.
- **Your own resentment:** Quiet resentment usually leaks out and damages the relationship more than a hard "no" would.

**Middle paths besides yes/no:**
- Help with a specific bill directly (rent, utilities) rather than cash.
- Set a cap — e.g., "I can do $X per year, that's it."
- Offer non-cash help: a place to stay short-term, help reworking a budget, job-search support, connecting him with resources.
- Make a loan formal — written terms, repayment schedule — even if you privately accept you may not be repaid.
- Have one honest conversation about what's actually going on in his life, separate from the immediate ask.

**A useful gut-check:** If you imagine saying no this time, what do you fear will happen? That fear is often more informative than the dollar amount — it tells you whether you're acting from generosity or from guilt/obligation.

There's no universally right answer. Family obligation is real, and so is your own financial and emotional sustainability. Whatever you decide, it's healthier when it's a deliberate choice rather than a reflex.
