# Anti-amyloid antibodies in early AD: calibrated answer

**Short answer:** Yes in a statistical sense, contested in a clinical-meaningfulness sense.

## What the pivotal trials showed

**Lecanemab — CLARITY-AD** (n=1795, 18 mo, early symptomatic AD with confirmed amyloid):
- CDR-SB change: 1.21 (drug) vs 1.66 (placebo). Absolute difference **−0.45**, ~27% relative slowing. p<0.001.
- ADAS-Cog14, ADCOMS, ADCS-MCI-ADL all moved in the same direction with similar effect sizes.

**Donanemab — TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2** (n=1736, 76 wk):
- In low/medium tau population: ~35% slowing on iADRS, ~36% on CDR-SB.
- Combined population: ~22–29% slowing. Plaque clearance was substantial; many participants stopped dosing on amyloid-PET negativity.

Both drugs replicated the amyloid-clearance → cognitive-slowing chain that prior anti-amyloid agents (bapineuzumab, solanezumab, aducanumab in EMERGE/ENGAGE) had failed to demonstrate convincingly. That is the strongest claim available: the amyloid hypothesis now has positive Phase 3 confirmatory evidence, twice.

## Why "effective" is contested

1. **Magnitude vs. minimal clinically important difference.** Common MCID estimates for CDR-SB in mild AD/MCI sit around 1–2 points. A −0.45 absolute difference at 18 months is below most MCID thresholds. Donanemab's ~0.7-point difference is closer but still inside the disputed band.
2. **It is slowing, not halting or reversing.** Both arms decline; the drug arm declines less.
3. **Subgroup heterogeneity.** APOE4 homozygotes show attenuated benefit and markedly higher ARIA risk. Lecanemab's pre-specified sex subgroup showed weaker point estimate in women; FDA/EMA reviewers flagged but did not treat as decisive.
4. **Safety is non-trivial.**
   - Lecanemab: ARIA-E 12.6%, ARIA-H 17.3%; symptomatic ARIA-E ~2.8%. Deaths in open-label extension, especially in APOE4/4 and anticoagulated patients.
   - Donanemab: ARIA-E ~24%, ARIA-H ~31%; 3 trial deaths attributed to ARIA.
5. **Brain-volume loss** in treated arms is greater than placebo — interpretation (pseudoatrophy from plaque/fluid clearance vs. true volume loss) is unresolved.
6. **Durability and dosing duration unknown.** Donanemab's stop-on-clearance design hints at finite dosing; lecanemab's needed duration is not established. Long-term (>2 yr) blinded data do not exist.
7. **Eligibility narrow.** MCI-to-mild-dementia stage, biomarker-confirmed amyloid, MRI-tolerant, manageable APOE4 risk, no anticoagulation conflict. Most diagnosed AD patients fall outside.

## Bottom line

- **Disease-modifying mechanism: supported.** Two independent Phase 3 wins on amyloid clearance + cognitive endpoints.
- **Patient-level clinical benefit: modest and below standard MCID thresholds**, with measurable harm rates that require MRI surveillance and APOE genotyping.
- **Reasonable clinician position:** offer to a narrow, well-counseled subset (early symptomatic, amyloid-confirmed, non-APOE4/4 or fully informed APOE4/4, no anticoagulation) where the patient values even a modest delay; not a default standard of care for all early AD.

Calling them "effective" without qualifying magnitude and harm overstates the evidence. Calling them ineffective ignores two positive confirmatory trials. The honest descriptor is **"modestly disease-modifying, with non-trivial safety burden, in a narrow population."**
