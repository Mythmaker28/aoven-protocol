This is genuinely hard, and there's no clean answer. A few things worth weighing honestly:

**Before deciding, pressure-test what you actually know.**
- How did you learn it? Direct observation, a confession from the cheater, a third-party rumor? The reliability gap between these is enormous.
- Could it be something that looks like cheating but isn't (open arrangement, separation you don't know about, misread context)?
- Are you certain enough that you'd stake the friendship on being right?

**Arguments for telling:**
- Your friend is being deprived of information needed to make decisions about their own life, health (STI risk), finances, and time.
- If they later learn you knew and stayed silent, that often damages the friendship more than telling does.
- Withholding can feel like a form of complicity.

**Arguments against telling, or for waiting:**
- Messengers frequently get blamed, even when right. Be ready for that.
- Your friend may already know, or suspect, or be in an arrangement you're not privy to.
- If the cheating spouse is the one you'd hear it from secondhand, your evidence may be thinner than it feels.

**A middle path some people take:**
Confront the cheating spouse first. Tell them you know and that they have a short window — a week, two weeks — to tell their partner themselves, or you will. This avoids ambushing your friend with secondhand information and forces the responsible party to own it. It also doesn't work if you don't trust them to actually do it, in which case you tell your friend directly.

**If you do tell:**
- In person, privately, sober, no audience.
- State what you know and how you know it. Don't editorialize or speculate beyond the facts.
- Make clear you're not telling them what to do with the information.
- Expect them to be angry — possibly at you — and don't take that as a signal you were wrong to tell.

The question I'd sit with: if the roles were reversed, would you want to know? Most people, when they answer that honestly, already know what they're going to do. The harder question is whether you can tolerate the cost of doing it.
