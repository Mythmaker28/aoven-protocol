This is a genuinely hard situation, and I don't think there's a single right answer. A few things worth thinking through before you decide:

**Questions to sit with first:**

- **How certain are you?** "Learned" can mean anything from witnessing it directly to hearing a third-hand rumor. The threshold for action should scale with the strength of your evidence. Acting on ambiguous information can do real damage.
- **What's your relationship to each party?** A close friend you've known for years is different from a casual acquaintance. Your obligations track the relationship.
- **How did you come to know?** If someone told you in confidence, you have a competing obligation. If you saw it yourself, that's different.
- **Is it ongoing, or a past event you've stumbled onto?** A current pattern your friend is making life decisions around (kids, finances, health) is more urgent than something resolved.
- **What would your friend want?** Most people, asked in the abstract, say they'd want to know — but in the moment, messengers often get blamed.

**Some considerations that often get overlooked:**

- **Confront the cheating spouse first.** A common middle path: tell the spouse you know and give them a short window (say, a week or two) to tell your friend themselves, or you will. This respects your friend's right to know while not making you the sole executioner of their marriage.
- **Beware of mixed motives.** If you have any romantic interest in your friend, dislike of the spouse, or stake in the outcome, that complicates things. Doesn't mean don't tell — but be honest with yourself.
- **Prepare for fallout.** Even when telling is the right call, friendships sometimes don't survive it. Your friend may associate you with the worst news of their life.
- **Tell, if you tell, with care.** In private, factually, without embellishment, and without pressuring them on what to do next. Then step back.

**My honest lean:** If the evidence is solid and the friendship is real, most ethicists and most people-after-the-fact land on "yes, tell" — but route it through the cheating spouse first if you can. The deception itself is what's harming your friend; you're not creating the harm by surfacing it.
