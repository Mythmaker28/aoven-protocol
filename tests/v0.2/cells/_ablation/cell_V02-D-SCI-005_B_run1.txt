# Anti-amyloid antibodies in early AD: calibrated read

**Short answer:** They produce statistically significant slowing of cognitive/functional decline on group-level scales, but the effect size is small, likely below most minimum clinically important difference (MCID) thresholds, and is bought with non-trivial harm. Whether that constitutes "effective" depends on the threshold you require.

## What the pivotal trials actually show

**Lecanemab — Clarity AD (van Dyck et al., NEJM 2023, n=1795, 18 mo)**
- CDR-SB: 1.21 (lecanemab) vs 1.66 (placebo); adjusted difference −0.45 (~27% slowing). p<0.001.
- ADAS-Cog14, ADCOMS, ADCS-MCI-ADL all significant in same direction.
- Amyloid PET cleared in most treated patients.

**Donanemab — TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 (Sims et al., JAMA 2023, n=1736, 76 wk)**
- iADRS: ~22–35% slowing depending on tau subgroup (low/medium tau population favored).
- CDR-SB difference ~−0.7.
- Stopped dosing once amyloid cleared (a meaningful design feature).

## Why "effective" deserves scare quotes

1. **MCID problem.** Commonly cited MCIDs for CDR-SB are ~1.0–2.5. Observed between-group deltas (~0.45–0.7) are well below. A patient or family is unlikely to detect the difference at the individual level over 18 months.
2. **Time-shift framing.** The benefit is roughly equivalent to a 4–7 month delay in reaching a given decline milestone, not a halt or reversal.
3. **Functional unblinding.** ARIA rates differ markedly between arms; infusion reactions (~26% lecanemab) are conspicuous. This plausibly contaminates rater/caregiver-reported endpoints.
4. **Harm is real.**
   - Lecanemab: ARIA-E 12.6%, ARIA-H 17.3%; symptomatic ARIA ~3%. Deaths in open-label extension associated with anticoagulation.
   - Donanemab: ARIA-E ~24%, ARIA-H ~31%; 3 trial deaths attributed to treatment.
   - **ApoE4 homozygotes** carry substantially higher ARIA risk; FDA labeling (and EMA's initial refusal, later qualified approval for non-homozygotes) reflects this.
5. **External validity.** Trial populations were highly selected (amyloid-positive, MCI/mild dementia, limited comorbidity, restricted anticoagulation). Real-world cohorts differ.
6. **Burden.** IV q2wk (lecanemab) or q4wk (donanemab), serial MRIs, ~$26K–$32K/yr drug cost, plus infrastructure.

## Where the evidence is genuinely strong

- **Biological target engagement is unambiguous.** Both clear amyloid plaques on PET, often to negative thresholds. This validates the amyloid hypothesis as *causally relevant* in a way prior failed trials had left open.
- **Disease-modification signal** (as opposed to symptomatic effect) is plausible given plaque clearance + sustained divergence of curves.
- Donanemab's tau-stratified design suggests benefit concentrates in earlier (low-tau) disease — consistent with the upstream biology.

## Bottom line

- **Group-level efficacy: yes, modest, replicated across two molecules.**
- **Individual-level clinically meaningful benefit: unproven and probably small for most patients.**
- **Net benefit-risk: defensible only with rigorous selection** (early disease, amyloid-confirmed, ApoE4 genotyping, no anticoagulation, MRI access, informed consent about modest expected benefit).
- **Open questions:** durability beyond 18–24 months, post-clearance dosing strategy, performance in ApoE4 homozygotes, and whether earlier (preclinical) treatment shifts the benefit-risk meaningfully — AHEAD 3-45 and TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 3 will inform.

Calling them "effective for early AD" is technically defensible against placebo on prespecified endpoints. Calling them "clinically transformative" is not supported by current data.
