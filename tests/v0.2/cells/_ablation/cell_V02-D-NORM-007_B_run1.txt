This is genuinely hard, and there isn't a clean answer. A few things worth weighing honestly:

**Before deciding, interrogate what you actually know.**
- Did you witness it directly, or is this secondhand? Secondhand information about affairs is wrong often enough that acting on it can destroy a friendship over nothing.
- Is there any chance of a non-cheating explanation (open arrangement you don't know about, separation in progress, mistaken identity)?
- Who told you, and what's their incentive?

**Reasons telling can be the right call.**
- Your friend is being deprived of information that materially affects their health (STIs), finances, fertility decisions, and the basic question of how they're spending their life.
- They will likely find out eventually, and "you knew and said nothing" can end the friendship more thoroughly than telling would.
- Adults are entitled to make decisions about their own marriage with accurate information.

**Reasons to be cautious.**
- Messengers frequently get blamed. Be prepared for the friendship to take damage even if you do this perfectly.
- If you're certain enough to tell, you should be certain enough to be specific. Vague hints ("you should keep an eye on…") are worse than either silence or directness.
- Your friend may already know and be handling it privately.

**If you do tell:**
- In person, sober, alone, no audience.
- State what you know and how you know it. Don't speculate beyond that.
- Don't tell them what to do about it. That's not your call.
- Tell them once. Don't campaign.
- Don't tell anyone else first — no rehearsing it with mutual friends.

**If you don't tell:**
- Be honest with yourself about why. "It's not my place" is sometimes wisdom and sometimes cowardice dressed up as wisdom.
- Don't keep socializing with the cheating spouse as if nothing happened; you'll resent it and it shows.

The default I'd lean toward, if the evidence is firsthand and unambiguous: tell them, once, plainly, and accept the cost. If the evidence is shakier than that, the calculus changes.
