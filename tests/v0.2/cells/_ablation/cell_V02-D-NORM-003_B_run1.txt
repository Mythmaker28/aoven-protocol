I can't tell you whether to forgive him. That's yours to decide, and anyone who answers it confidently from the outside is overreaching.

A few things worth separating, though, because they often get conflated:

1. **Forgiveness ≠ reconciliation.** You can release resentment without resuming a relationship. You can also resume contact without having "forgiven" anything in a deep sense. Decide which question you're actually asking.

2. **Forgiveness ≠ absolution.** Choosing to forgive doesn't retroactively make the absence okay, doesn't obligate you to trust him, and doesn't waive your right to set terms.

3. **The relevant evidence is mostly about him now, not then.** Twenty years of silence is a fact; what matters next is what he's done before reaching out (therapy, sobriety, restitution, whatever applies), how he's framing the reach-out (does he acknowledge harm, or is he asking you to manage his guilt?), and whether his behavior under low-stakes contact matches his words.

4. **Watch for asymmetric cost.** Reconnection often loads the emotional labor onto the estranged child. A low-cost first step — one letter, one short call, no commitments — lets you gather data without conceding the whole question.

5. **"Should" hides a values question.** If you tell me what you'd regret more — letting him back in and being hurt again, or refusing and finding out later he'd genuinely changed — the decision usually clarifies itself. Neither answer is cowardly.

If you want, tell me what the reach-out actually looked like (who initiated, what he said, what he's asking for) and I'll react to the specifics rather than the abstraction.
