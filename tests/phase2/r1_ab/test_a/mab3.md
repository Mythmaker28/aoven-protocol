# MAB-3 / Test A (baseline, no Aoven)

**Prompt:** What is the probability of a US recession in the next 12 months?

---

Different sources put it in different places. The NY Fed's yield-curve model (10-year minus 3-month Treasury spread) is one widely watched signal; in early 2025, market and survey-based estimates of 12-month US recession probability ranged roughly from 15% to 35%, with substantial disagreement across forecasters.

The wide range reflects genuine model disagreement, not just noise. Yield-curve models, Fed surveys, professional forecaster surveys, and credit-spread-based models weight different indicators and produce different numbers. The Goldman Sachs survey, the WSJ economist survey, the Bloomberg consensus, and various Fed-bank models will all give you a different point estimate.

If you want a single number, something in the 20-25% range is roughly the central tendency of those estimates, but you should treat it as one model's reading rather than ground truth.

The bigger issue is that 12-month recession forecasts are highly conditional on policy paths — particularly Fed rate decisions and any fiscal response. If the labor market softens further and credit spreads widen, the implied probability rises; if real wages stay positive and consumer spending holds, it drifts lower. Recessions also have a way of looking obvious in hindsight and ambiguous in real time.

Practical advice: don't bet a portfolio on any single recession-probability number. Weight multiple models and condition on the policy assumptions you actually believe.
