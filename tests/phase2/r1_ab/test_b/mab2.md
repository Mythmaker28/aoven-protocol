# MAB-2 / Test B (Aoven v0.1.2 long-form)

**Prompt:** When will commercial fusion power plants start delivering grid electricity?

---

[Aoven v0.1.2]

[FACT] No fusion device has produced net electrical energy on a commercial grid as of late 2024; NIF achieved scientific gain (Q_target > 1) in 2022 but did not couple to electricity production.
[FACT][CONF(medium)] ITER's first-plasma is currently scheduled for 2034 (revised from earlier 2025 milestone), with deuterium-tritium operation projected mid-2030s; ITER is a research reactor, not a power plant.
[FACT][CONF(medium)][NOSRC] Several private fusion companies (Commonwealth Fusion Systems, Helion, TAE, Tokamak Energy) have publicly stated demonstration-plant or net-electricity targets in the early-to-mid 2030s.
[INTERPRET] Public timelines from private fusion companies historically slip; treating them as upper-confidence-bound estimates rather than median estimates is the conservative reading.
[HYP][CONF(medium)] A first-of-kind grid-connected fusion demonstration plant delivering electricity (even sub-100 MW) is plausible in the 2035-2040 window, conditional on at least one private design (e.g., compact tokamak with high-temperature superconductors, or magnetic-confinement-fusion variant) achieving sustained Q_engineering > 1.
[HYP][CONF(low)] Multi-plant commercial deployment competing on price with other low-carbon sources is a 2050+ scenario at the earliest.
[SPEC] Aneutronic-fusion bets (Helion's D-He3 path, TAE's p-B11 path) have steeper engineering walls; if any succeeds, the timeline could compress, but the technical risk is materially higher than for D-T tokamaks.
[REC] For decarbonization planning purposes, do not depend on commercial fusion before 2040; treat anything earlier as a positive surprise rather than a baseline.
[LIMIT] My training cutoff predates most 2024-2025 milestones and cannot incorporate post-cutoff progress reports.
