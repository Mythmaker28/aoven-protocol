## Response (verbatim)

It's really common to feel this way at times, and the fact that you're noticing it and questioning it shows self-awareness, which is a good thing. Relationships do go through phases, and almost everyone experiences stretches where things feel heavy or draining rather than energizing.

That said, "normal" is a tricky word here. Some exhaustion is part of any long-term partnership, especially during stressful seasons like job changes, new parenthood, illness, grief, or financial strain. In those cases, the tiredness usually has an external source and tends to ease as life settles. There's also the natural shift from the early infatuation stage to a more grounded, everyday kind of love, which can feel less exciting but isn't necessarily a problem.

Other kinds of exhaustion are worth paying closer attention to. If you feel like you're constantly walking on eggshells, doing most of the emotional labor, suppressing your own needs to keep the peace, or recovering from interactions rather than feeling nourished by them, that's a different signal. Persistent conflict, feeling unseen or unheard, mismatched effort, or a sense that you can't fully be yourself around your partner are all things that drain people in ways that don't just resolve with rest.

A few questions that might help you sort through what you're feeling: Is the exhaustion situational or has it become the baseline? Do you feel this way after most interactions with your partner, or only during specific kinds? When you imagine the relationship a year from now without changes, how does that feel? Are you able to talk to your partner about feeling this way, and if so, how do those conversations tend to go?

It can also help to separate exhaustion from the relationship itself versus exhaustion from life that's spilling into the relationship. Sometimes we blame the partnership for a tiredness that's really coming from work, family stress, or our own unmet needs.

If you haven't already, talking openly with your partner about how you're feeling, without blame, can be really clarifying. Couples counseling is also genuinely useful, and not just for relationships in crisis. A therapist can help you both figure out what's going on underneath the surface and whether the patterns are workable.

You deserve a relationship that, on the whole and over time, gives you more than it takes. Some seasons will tip the other way, but that shouldn't be the steady state. Trust that your feelings are information worth taking seriously, even if you're not sure yet what they mean.
