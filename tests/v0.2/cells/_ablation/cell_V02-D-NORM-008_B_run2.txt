# On honesty here

Short answer: the framing of your question contains a false binary. The real choice isn't "honest vs. dishonest" — it's *what* you're being honest *about*.

## What you should not say

"You're not very talented" — even if you believe it — fails on three grounds:

1. **It's probably not knowable at 12.** Talent assessments at this age are notoriously poor predictors. Late bloomers in music are well-documented; the neurological and physical maturation that affects musicianship continues into the late teens. Hilary Hahn was unusual; most pros didn't look like prodigies at 12.
2. **It conflates two different claims.** "You lack talent" sounds like a fixed verdict. What you likely mean is "your current trajectory doesn't suggest a top-tier professional career" — which is a probabilistic statement about a brutally competitive field, not a judgment of her.
3. **You're not a qualified evaluator** unless you're a professional string player or teacher. Your assessment is a guess.

## What honesty actually requires

Honesty about the **field**, not the **child**:

- Professional classical violin is one of the most savagely competitive careers that exists. Even genuinely talented, hardworking conservatory graduates often don't make it. This is true regardless of her ability.
- Ask her teacher — directly and privately — for a candid read. Not "is she talented?" but "where does she stand relative to students who go on to conservatory auditions?" Teachers will usually tell you if pressed.
- If she's serious, the honest move is to get her in front of better teachers and harder environments (youth orchestras, summer programs, masterclasses). Reality will calibrate her faster and more accurately than you can.

## The deeper question

You're asking whether to manage her dream. Consider that you don't have to. A 12-year-old saying "I want to be a professional violinist" is not signing a contract — she's expressing identity and desire. Most kids' dreams shift. The ones that don't, survive contact with reality on their own.

Your job isn't to puncture the dream or inflate it. It's to give her honest information about the **path** (what conservatory admissions look like, what a working violinist's life is, what the odds are) and let her recalibrate against that.

If she's still aiming for it at 16 after seeing what the path actually demands, that tells you something her current ability doesn't.
