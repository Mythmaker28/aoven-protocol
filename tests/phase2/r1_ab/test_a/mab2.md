# MAB-2 / Test A (baseline, no Aoven)

**Prompt:** When will commercial fusion power plants start delivering grid electricity?

---

No fusion device has produced net electrical energy on a commercial grid yet. The 2022 NIF result demonstrated scientific gain (more fusion energy out than laser energy delivered to the target), but that's far from net electricity at the wall plug.

ITER, the major international research project, is now targeting first plasma in 2034 with full deuterium-tritium operation in the late 2030s. ITER is not a power plant; it's a research facility designed to demonstrate sustained burning plasma. A commercial follow-on (DEMO) would be a 2050s project.

The interesting wildcard is the private sector. Companies like Commonwealth Fusion Systems (high-temperature superconducting tokamak), Helion (D-He3 with direct conversion), TAE Technologies, and Tokamak Energy have publicly stated targets for net-electricity demonstration in the early-to-mid 2030s. Private fusion timelines historically slip — a lot — but the field has genuinely accelerated since around 2020 thanks to high-temperature superconductors and substantial capital inflows.

A realistic baseline: a first-of-kind grid-connected demonstration plant somewhere in 2035-2040 if at least one private design pans out, multi-plant commercial deployment competing on price probably 2050+. Anyone planning grid decarbonization on a 10-15 year horizon should not depend on commercial fusion; treat earlier arrival as positive surprise.

Aneutronic-fusion bets (Helion's D-He3, TAE's p-B11) face steeper engineering walls but would change the picture if successful.
